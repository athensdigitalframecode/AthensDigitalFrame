Act as an internationally recognised creative director, award-winning digital designer, senior frontend developer, 3D interaction designer, motion designer, UX/UI specialist, and digital brand strategist.

Your task is to transform the existing Athens Digital Frame website into an exceptionally sophisticated, distinctive, premium digital studio experience.

This is not a simple website refresh. It is a complete creative and technical transformation of an existing business website.

The project has FOUR major objectives:

1. Research and learn from the world's best award-winning digital studios and interactive websites before defining the creative direction.
2. Completely redesign the existing website with an original, premium visual identity and outstanding user experience.
3. Design, build, and integrate a brand-new interactive 3D cube from scratch, featuring the official Athens Digital Frame logo and showcasing the studio's six core services.
4. Deliver a complete, responsive, high-performance website that not only looks internationally competitive but also attracts potential clients and generates genuine business enquiries.

CRITICAL: There is currently NO 3D cube implementation. You must create the entire 3D cube from scratch. Do not assume one already exists, and do not substitute it with a static image, video, or simple 2D animation.

The final result must be a fully functional website implemented directly inside the existing project.

## 4. CREATE A BRANDED INTERACTIVE 3D CUBE FROM SCRATCH

The 3D cube is one of the most important elements of the entire website and should become a distinctive visual signature of Athens Digital Frame.

Build a genuine interactive six-sided 3D cube from scratch.

The cube must prominently feature the official Athens Digital Frame logo as its primary identity, while its six faces showcase the studio's six core services.

The cube should feel like a carefully designed physical brand object, not a generic rotating geometric shape.

Choose the most appropriate implementation for the existing project architecture.

CSS 3D transforms, Three.js, React Three Fiber, or another suitable solution may be considered.

Prioritise:

* Visual quality.
* Accurate 3D geometry.
* Smooth interaction.
* Performance.
* Maintainability.
* Responsive rendering.

### 4.1 Default state — Official Athens Digital Frame logo

When the website initially loads, the cube should prominently display the official Athens Digital Frame logo on its front-facing surface.

Requirements:

* Use the actual existing logo asset from the project.
* Do not recreate, approximate, distort, or replace the logo.
* Preserve its original proportions and colours.
* Integrate the logo naturally into the cube's surface design.
* Ensure it is immediately recognisable.
* Give the cube a sophisticated dimensional appearance.
* Use carefully considered materials, lighting, shadows, reflections, and depth.
* Make the logo the main focus of the initial cube presentation.

The logo should be integrated into the cube's actual visual design, not simply placed as an unrelated overlay.

Design the resting state so the logo is the dominant visual element.

As the visitor interacts with the cube, transition naturally from the logo presentation into the service showcase.

The logo does not constitute a seventh face. The cube must have exactly six physical faces, each representing one of the six core services.

The logo can be presented as an integrated branded layer or treatment on the front-facing surface, transitioning to reveal the service identity beneath it. Maintain subtle brand details throughout the other faces as well.

### 4.2 The six cube faces — Showcase our services

The cube must have six properly constructed, correctly oriented physical faces.

Each face represents one core service:

1. DISCOVER
2. DESIGN
3. DEVELOP
4. E-COMMERCE
5. VISIBILITY
6. SUPPORT

Each face should have:

* A clearly visible service title.
* Carefully designed typography.
* A distinctive graphical composition.
* A subtle connection to the studio's logo and identity.
* A unique but consistent visual treatment.
* Proper spacing and alignment.
* A sophisticated presentation that remains readable at different angles.

Do not simply place six identical labels on flat panels.

Design each face as part of a unified visual object.

The cube must have accurate geometry, realistic perspective, properly aligned faces, believable depth, and consistent proportions.

It must look genuinely three-dimensional from every angle.

### 4.3 Hover interaction — Make the cube come alive

This interaction is particularly important.

When a visitor moves their mouse over the cube, it should come alive and begin an elegant sequence of rotations.

The cube should rotate in different directions across its X, Y, and Z axes, revealing the six service faces in an engaging way.

The experience should feel like exploring a physical, precision-engineered brand object.

Implement:

* Smooth multi-directional rotation.
* Natural rotational acceleration and deceleration.
* Controlled rotational inertia.
* Fluid transitions between faces.
* Brief pauses at important viewing angles.
* Smooth and readable service reveals.
* Appropriate rotation sensitivity.
* No uncontrolled or excessively fast spinning.

The cube should showcase all six services through intentional rotations rather than constantly spinning without purpose.

The animation should be interesting enough to encourage interaction while remaining sophisticated and professional.

When the cursor leaves the cube, smoothly return it to the original logo-facing resting position, provided the visitor has not deliberately selected or pinned a service.

Do not make the logo disappear permanently after the first interaction.

The branded resting position must always be recoverable.

### 4.4 Manual interaction

Users should be able to control the cube themselves.

Implement:

* Mouse dragging.
* Touch dragging.
* Selecting individual faces.
* Bringing the selected service face forward.
* Smooth snapping to the correct orientation.
* Pause and resume controls where appropriate.
* Natural movement and rotational inertia.

Allow visitors to explore the cube instead of forcing them to follow a predetermined animation.

Provide clear controls to select any of the six services directly.

When a service is selected, rotate the cube to the correct face and synchronise the website's service information.

Prevent conflicting automatic and manual animations.

A deliberate selection should remain active until the user changes or resets it.

### 4.5 Service descriptions — Connect the cube to the website

The cube and surrounding website interface must work together as one experience.

When a service face becomes active:

* Display the service title outside the cube.
* Show a concise description of what Athens Digital Frame does.
* Update the active service indicator.
* Smoothly transition between descriptions.
* Maintain a clear visual connection between the displayed text and the corresponding cube face.

Use the following descriptions:

01 — DISCOVER
We get to know your business, audience, and goals to uncover opportunities and define the right digital direction.

02 — DESIGN
We create distinctive visual identities and thoughtful user interfaces, combining creativity, usability, and attention to detail.

03 — DEVELOP
We turn ideas into responsive, high-performance websites built with modern technologies and carefully considered functionality.

04 — E-COMMERCE
We build tailored online shopping experiences that make purchasing simple, improve customer journeys, and support business growth.

05 — VISIBILITY
We strengthen your online presence through SEO foundations, search optimisation, and strategies that help your business get discovered.

06 — SUPPORT
We provide ongoing maintenance, technical assistance, improvements, and guidance to keep your digital presence working smoothly.

These descriptions should be presented elegantly beside or underneath the cube, depending on screen size and layout.

Do not permanently display all six descriptions over the cube.

Reveal the relevant description as visitors explore each service.

Use subtle opacity, positioning, and transition effects to create a polished experience.

### 4.6 Complete cube interaction sequence

The intended interaction should work as follows:

Initial state: The visitor sees a beautifully rendered 3D cube with the official Athens Digital Frame logo facing forward.

Mouse hover: The cube comes alive and rotates smoothly in different directions.

Service reveal: Each rotation presents one of the six services.

Information update: The corresponding service title and description appear in the website interface.

Direct interaction: Visitors can drag the cube or select specific services themselves.

Manual selection: The selected service face becomes active and remains selected until changed or reset.

Mouse exit: The cube smoothly returns to its original logo-facing position only when no deliberate service selection is active.

Mobile: Visitors can reproduce the experience through touch interactions, tapping, and accessible service-selection controls.

The objective is to make visitors curious enough to interact with the cube and discover what Athens Digital Frame does through the experience itself.

### 4.7 3D appearance and rendering quality

Pay particular attention to:

* Accurate cube geometry.
* Correctly oriented faces.
* Realistic depth and perspective.
* Sophisticated lighting and shading.
* Subtle reflections and material details.
* Refined edges and surface treatments.
* Consistent proportions.
* Visually balanced typography.
* High-quality rendering.

Avoid cartoonish appearances, excessive glow, random spinning, unnecessary particles, and exaggerated effects.

The cube should feel like a premium digital brand sculpture.

### 4.8 Responsive implementation

The cube must work beautifully on:

Desktop

* Large, visually prominent cube.
* Natural mouse interaction.
* Sophisticated lighting and perspective.
* Balanced relationship with hero typography.

Tablet

* Adapted dimensions and positioning.
* Touch-friendly interaction.
* Correctly scaled service content.

Mobile

* Appropriately reduced dimensions.
* Optimised touch interactions.
* No overflow or layout collisions.
* Readable service labels.
* Clear controls for selecting each service.
* Performance-conscious rendering.

Provide an appropriate fallback when WebGL is unavailable or rendering performance is insufficient.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.

The service information must remain accessible even when the 3D experience is unavailable.

Do not consider the cube complete until the actual functional 3D implementation is integrated into the website and tested.

## 20. SECURITY, PRIVACY AND RELIABILITY

The website must be secure and reliable as a real business website, not just visually impressive.

Inspect existing security, privacy, and data-handling functionality before making changes.

Preserve and improve existing protections where appropriate.

### 20.1 Contact form security

* Validate all submitted data on the server wherever a backend is available.
* Sanitise and validate user input appropriately.
* Implement appropriate spam protection.
* Use rate limiting where supported.
* Prevent malicious or unexpected input from being processed.
* Provide safe and helpful error messages.
* Never expose sensitive form submissions or customer information unintentionally.

Do not rely exclusively on frontend validation for security.

### 20.2 Credentials and integrations

* Never expose API keys, private credentials, access tokens, or secrets in frontend code.
* Use environment variables and secure server-side configuration where appropriate.
* Avoid committing secrets to the repository.
* Inspect existing third-party integrations before changing them.
* Do not introduce unnecessary external services or dependencies.
* Handle failed API requests and integration errors gracefully.

### 20.3 Privacy and cookies

* Preserve existing privacy policies and cookie-consent functionality.
* Preserve existing privacy-related configurations and integrations.
* Do not introduce unnecessary tracking scripts or analytics services.
* Do not add unnecessary cookie banners if no additional functionality requires them.
* Ensure any existing analytics or tracking functionality respects the site's privacy and consent requirements.
* Do not collect more personal data than necessary.
* Make contact form data handling transparent and appropriate.

### 20.4 Reliability and error handling

* Handle failed form submissions gracefully.
* Avoid exposing technical errors or sensitive server information to visitors.
* Ensure external services and integrations fail gracefully.
* Prevent broken navigation or unexpected application crashes.
* Avoid unnecessary dependencies that introduce security or maintenance risks.
* Preserve existing working functionality during implementation.

* Test contact form validation.
* Verify spam-protection mechanisms where available.
